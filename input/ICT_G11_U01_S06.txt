Key idea: The flow links inputs, processing, outputs, and feedback to real applications such as banking, government, learning, and ERP or CRM.

Lesson content (authoritative — stay accurate and do not add facts beyond it):
Electronic Banking (E-banking): The delivery of banking services through electronic channels, primarily the internet and mobile networks. In Ethiopia, the adoption of mobile banking has been remarkable. CBE Birr, Dashen Bank's Amole service, and TeleBirr have together brought millions of previously unbanked Ethiopians into the formal financial system. Beyond consumer services, e-banking includes interbank electronic fund transfer systems and automated clearing houses.

Electronic Government (E-government): The use of digital technologies to deliver government services more efficiently, transparently, and conveniently.

Electronic Learning (E-learning): Education and training delivered or supported through electronic media. E-learning platforms allow students to access lecture videos, interactive exercises, quizzes, and discussion forums from any location with an internet connection. Ethiopia's EduTV programme broadcasts educational content via satellite television.

Electronic Health (E-health): ICT — Information and Communication Technology — used to support and improve healthcare delivery. Applications include Electronic Health Records (EHR) systems, telemedicine platforms allowing patients in remote areas to consult with doctors in cities, and Health Management Information Systems (HMIS) that aggregate disease surveillance data.

Cambridge Perspective — Cambridge AS Level IT 9626: The Cambridge syllabus identifies seven key characteristics of good information: accuracy (free from errors), completeness (all required data present), timeliness (available when needed), relevance (appropriate for the task), accessibility (reachable by authorised users), clarity (presented in an understandable format), and cost-effectiveness (value exceeds cost of obtaining).

ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) integrates all core business processes — finance, HR, procurement, production, inventory, and customer relations — into a single unified software platform with a shared database. Major ERP vendors include SAP, Oracle, and Microsoft Dynamics. In Ethiopia, Ethiopian Airlines and Ethio Telecom use ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning systems.
