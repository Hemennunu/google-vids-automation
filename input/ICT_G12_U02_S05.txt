Lesson content (authoritative — stay accurate and do not add facts beyond it):
Cloud Computing is a paradigm for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources including networks, servers, storage, applications, and services. Rather than owning and maintaining their own physical data centres and servers, organisations can rent access to computing resources from cloud service providers on a pay-as-you-go basis. This model fundamentally transforms the economics of information technology by converting capital expenditure on hardware into variable operational expenditure. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines five essential characteristics of cloud computing: on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, and measured service.

The benefits of cloud computing are substantial and well-documented. Organisations can achieve significant cost savings by eliminating the need to purchase, maintain, and upgrade expensive hardware. Cloud services offer virtually unlimited scalability, allowing businesses to scale their computing resources up or down in response to demand.

Cloud computing is typically categorised into three primary service models. Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) provides virtualised computing resources over the internet, including virtual machines, storage, and networking. Users have control over operating systems, storage, and deployed applications but do not manage the underlying cloud infrastructure.
