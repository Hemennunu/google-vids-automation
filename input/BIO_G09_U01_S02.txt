Lesson content (authoritative — stay accurate and do not add facts beyond it):
Unit 1: Introduction to Biology serves as the foundational gateway to the entire Grade 9 Biology curriculum. This unit is designed to establish a solid conceptual framework for all subsequent biological study by introducing students to the fundamental nature of biology as a scientific discipline, the reasons why the study of life is important, the systematic methods biologists use to investigate the living world, the essential tools employed in biological research, and the critical safety practices that must be observed in any laboratory setting. The unit is organised around six interconnected sub-topics that progress logically from broad conceptual understanding to practical application. 6).

The six sub-topics covered in this unit are carefully sequenced to build knowledge cumulatively.

This unit is designed to be delivered over a period of ten to twelve class periods, each of approximately forty minutes in duration, though teachers may adjust the pace according to the needs of their students and the availability of laboratory resources. The unit assumes no prior formal study of biology, though students are expected to have a basic foundational knowledge of general science as acquired in earlier grades.

As the first unit of the Grade 9 Biology curriculum, this introduction plays a critical role in setting the stage for the more specialised topics that follow. Later units will build upon the foundational concepts established here, exploring cell biology (Unit 2), human biology and health (Unit 3), genetics (Unit 4), ecology and conservation (Unit 5), and the diversity of living organisms (Unit 6).

Ethiopian context to feature (use these specific places, species and initiatives, not generic African imagery):
- This unit is particularly relevant to Ethiopian students because it provides the scientific vocabulary and conceptual tools needed to understand and appreciate the extraordinary biological heritage of their own country. Ethiopia is one of the eight major centres of genetic diversity in the world for crop plants, and it is the centre of origin for coffee (Coffea arabica), one of the most economically important crops globally. Understanding the basic principles of biology introduced in this unit will enable students to engage more meaningfully with Ethiopia's biodiversity conservation efforts, including the work of Addis Ababa University's Department of Biology and the Ethiopian Public Health Institute (EPHI) in studying and protecting the nation's living resources.
