Lesson content (authoritative — stay accurate and do not add facts beyond it):
The twenty-first century has witnessed an extraordinary acceleration in the development and deployment of information and communication technologies that are reshaping every dimension of human life. Unit 2 of the Grade 12 Information Technology syllabus, entitled "Emerging Technologies," invites students to explore four of the most transformative technological domains of our time: Big Data, Cloud Computing, Fog Computing, and the Internet of Things (IoT). These technologies are not isolated innovations; they are deeply interconnected and together form the backbone of the digital transformation sweeping across industries, governments, and societies worldwide.

Big Data refers to the vast volumes of structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data that are generated every second from sources such as social media, sensors, financial transactions, and mobile devices. The ability to capture, store, process, and analyse this data has become a critical competitive advantage for organisations.

The unit is structured to build conceptual understanding progressively. Students begin by examining the defining characteristics of Big Data, known as the four Vs: volume, velocity, variety, and veracity.

Throughout the unit, worked examples and practical investigations enable students to apply theoretical concepts to realistic scenarios. A Cambridge AS Level extension section provides additional depth for students pursuing advanced study, and the unit concludes with a comprehensive summary that reinforces key learning points.

Ethiopian context to feature (use these specific places, species and initiatives, not generic African imagery):
- 🇪🇩 Ethiopian Context Ethiopia stands at a critical juncture in its digital transformation journey. The government's Digital Ethiopia 2025 strategy has catalysed investments in national broadband infrastructure, digital financial services through Telebirr and other mobile money platforms, and e-government services. Ethio Telecom, the state-owned telecommunications provider, manages vast datasets generated by more than seventy million subscribers. The Ethiopian Commodity Exchange (ECX) processes terabytes of transaction data daily, tracking coffee, sesame, and other agricultural commodities from farm to export. In the agricultural sector, which employs over seventy percent of the population, IoT sensors and cloud-based analytics are beginning to transform traditional farming practices by providing real-time weather data, soil moisture monitoring, and market price information.
