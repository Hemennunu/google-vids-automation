## LMS LESSON (authoritative)
LEAD

The Internet of Things refers to the network of physical objects, or "things," that are embedded with sensors, software, and other technologies to connect and exchange data with other devices and systems over the internet. The architecture of an IoT system can be understood as a layered structure comprising four fundamental components. The perception layer consists of sensors and actuators that interact with the physical environment; sensors collect data such as temperature, humidity, motion, or light levels, while actuators perform physical actions such as opening a valve or switching on a motor. The network layer transmits data from sensors to processing systems using communication protocols such as Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, LoRaWAN, or cellular networks. The processing layer analyses the data using cloud or fog computing resources, applying analytics algorithms to extract meaningful insights. Finally, the application layer delivers the results to end users through dashboards, mobile applications, or automated control systems, enabling informed decision-making or autonomous action.

PARAGRAPHS

By 2025, it is estimated that there will be more than 30 billion IoT-connected devices worldwide, generating over 79 zettabytes of data annually. In Ethiopia, the expansion of the 4G network and the impending rollout of 5G technology are expected to accelerate IoT adoption across agriculture, healthcare, transportation, and manufacturing sectors.

In the healthcare sector, IoT is enabling remote patient monitoring, telemedicine, and improved healthcare delivery in both urban and rural settings. IoT-enabled medical devices can continuously monitor patient vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose levels, transmitting data to healthcare providers who can intervene when abnormalities are detected. This is particularly valuable in Ethiopia, where the doctor-to-patient ratio remains among the lowest in Africa, and where many patients in rural areas must travel long distances to access healthcare facilities. Telemedicine platforms powered by IoT devices allow specialists in Addis Ababa to consult with patients and healthcare workers in remote health centres, bridging the gap in access to specialised medical expertise. Cold chain monitoring systems using IoT temperature sensors ensure that vaccines and medicines are stored within required temperature ranges throughout the distribution network, a critical capability for Ethiopia's immunisation programmes.

Ethiopian cities, particularly Addis Ababa, are exploring smart city applications of IoT to improve urban services and quality of life. Smart traffic management systems using IoT sensors and cameras can monitor traffic flow in real time, adjusting traffic signal timing to reduce congestion and emissions. Smart street lighting systems equipped with motion sensors can reduce electricity consumption by dimming lights when no pedestrians or vehicles are present. Waste management systems using fill-level sensors on garbage bins can optimise collection routes, reducing fuel consumption and improving city cleanliness. The Addis Ababa City Administration has begun deploying IoT-based environmental monitoring stations to measure air quality, noise levels, and weather conditions across different parts of the city, providing data that informs public health and urban planning decisions. These initiatives, while still in their early stages, illustrate the transformative potential of IoT for Ethiopian urban development.

ETHIOPIAN CONTEXT

🇪🇩 Ethiopian Context Agriculture remains the backbone of the Ethiopian economy, contributing approximately 35 percent of the country's gross domestic product and employing more than 70 percent of the population. IoT technologies are beginning to transform this sector through precision agriculture applications. Soil moisture sensors deployed across farm fields can transmit real-time data to farmers via mobile applications, enabling them to optimise irrigation schedules and conserve water resources. Weather stations equipped with IoT sensors provide hyperlocal temperature, rainfall, and wind speed data that inform planting and harvesting decisions. Livestock monitoring systems using GPS-enabled collars and health sensors allow pastoralist communities to track the location, movement patterns, and health status of their herds, reducing losses from disease and theft. The Ethiopian Ministry of Agriculture has piloted IoT-based early warning systems that monitor crop health using satellite imagery and ground-level sensors, enabling rapid response to pest infestations such as desert locust swarms. These applications demonstrate how IoT can increase agricultural productivity, reduce resource waste, and improve food security for millions of Ethiopian smallholder farmers.

2.4.2 How Does IoT Work?
A complete IoT system integrates four component.
1. Sensors/devices- First, sensors or devices collect data from their
environment. Multiple sensors can be bundled together or sensors can
be part of a device that does more than just sense things. For example,
smart mobile phone is a device that may have multiple sensors (camera,
accelerometer, etc.), but your phone is not just a sensor. There are plenty of
sensor types which can be used based on the purpose.
• Examples includes temperature sensors, proximity sensors, humidity
sensors, smoke sensors, water quality sensors, infrared sensors, motion
sensors etc.
2. Connectivity- The collected data is sent to the cloud through a variety of
methods including cellular, satellite, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or connecting di-
rectly to the Internet via Ethernet. Choosing which connectivity option as-
sociated to the specific IoT application required.
3. Data Processing- The software processes the data after it is transferred
to the cloud. This could be very simple, like making sure the temperature
reading is within permissible limits, or highly complicated, like utilizing
computer vision to detect things in the video (like intruders in your house).
But what happens if the temperature gets too high or if someone breaks into
your home? The users might steps in at this point as required.

cloud storage via an app on their phone, or system could take actions

Key Terms
Big Data - refers to complex and large data sets that have to be processed
and analyzed to uncover valuable information that can benefit businesses and
organizations
Cloud Computing - The distribution of various services, such as data storage,
servers, databases, networking, and software, through the Internet
Fog Computing - is a decentralized computing infrastructure in which
data, compute, storage and applications are located somewhere between the
data source and the cloud.
Infrastructure as a Service- is a form of cloud computing that provides
virtualized computing resources over the internet.
Internet - a global computer network providing a variety of information
and communication facilities, consisting of interconnected networks using
standardized communication protocols.
Internet of Things - describes physical objects with sensors, processing
ability, software, and other technologies that connect and exchange data
with other devices and systems over the Internet or other communications
networks.
Platform as a Service - is a type of cloud computing that offers users a
whole cloud platform, including hardware, software, and infrastructure, for
creating, administering, and using applications.
Software as a Service - is a way of delivering applications over the
Internet—as a service where users can avoid complicated software and device
maintenance by just accessing software via the Internet rather than installing
and maintaining it.
