## LMS LESSON (authoritative)
LEAD

Data science is an interdisciplinary field combining statistics, computer programming, and domain-specific expertise to extract meaningful insights from structured and unstructured data.

PARAGRAPHS

Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the simulation of human intelligence processes by computer systems, including learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, and language understanding. AI has transitioned from a theoretical concept to a transformative force reshaping industries, professions, and daily life worldwide.

Machine learning (ML) is the dominant paradigm within AI, enabling systems to learn from data rather than being explicitly programmed. In supervised learning, a model is trained on labelled examples and learns to predict outputs for new inputs. In unsupervised learning, the model discovers patterns in unlabelled data. In reinforcement learning, an agent learns by interacting with an environment and receiving rewards or penalties for its actions. Deep learning, using artificial neural networks with many layers, has achieved remarkable results in image recognition, natural language processing, and game-playing.

Data science is the interdisciplinary field that extracts insights from large and complex datasets using statistical methods, machine learning, and computational tools. The data science workflow includes problem formulation, data collection, data cleaning and preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, model building, evaluation, and deployment. Python is the primary programming language, with key libraries including Pandas, Matplotlib, Scikit-learn, and TensorFlow.

The Internet of Things (IoT) connects physical devices — sensors, appliances, vehicles — to the internet, enabling data collection and exchange. Smart agriculture applications use soil moisture sensors, weather stations, and drone imagery to optimise farming, directly relevant to Ethiopia's agricultural development challenges. Blockchain technology creates distributed, immutable ledgers without a trusted central authority; the Ethiopian government has partnered with IOHK to create blockchain-based digital student identity cards.

Ethical considerations are inseparable from AI deployment. Algorithmic bias occurs when AI systems reflect or amplify biases present in training data, leading to discriminatory outcomes. Privacy concerns arise from massive data collection requirements. Job displacement from automation requires policy responses in education and social protection. Responsible AI development requires transparency, fairness, accountability, and human oversight — principles that must guide Ethiopia's digital transformation.

The data science lifecycle provides a structured framework for conducting data science projects, from initial problem formulation to final deployment and monitoring. The lifecycle typically begins with problem formulation, where data scientists collaborate with domain experts to clearly define the business or research question to be addressed. The second phase involves data collection and acquisition, gathering raw data from various sources such as databases, APIs, web scraping, sensors, surveys, or publicly available datasets. In Ethiopia, data sources might include Ministry of Health records, agricultural surveys from the Central Statistical Agency, mobile phone transaction logs from Ethio Telecom, or satellite imagery from remote sensing platforms.

Data cleaning and preprocessing follows, often the most time-consuming phase, where data scientists handle missing values, correct inconsistencies, remove duplicates, and transform data into a usable format. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) then uses summary statistics and visualisation techniques to understand patterns, relationships, and anomalies in the data. The modelling phase applies statistical or machine learning algorithms to build predictive or descriptive models. Model evaluation assesses performance using appropriate metrics, and deployment integrates the model into production systems. The final phase, monitoring and maintenance, ensures the model continues to perform well over time and is updated as new data becomes available.

Big data refers to extremely large and complex datasets that cannot be easily managed, processed, or analysed using traditional data processing tools. Big data is characterised by four dimensions known as the four Vs: Volume (the sheer quantity of data generated), Velocity (the speed at which data is generated and must be processed), Variety (the different types of data including structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data from sources like text, images, video, and sensor readings), and Veracity (the quality and trustworthiness of the data). Common tools used in data science include Python programming language with libraries such as Pandas for data manipulation, Matplotlib and Seaborn for visualisation, Scikit-learn for machine learning, and TensorFlow and PyTorch for deep learning. For big data processing, technologies such as Apache Hadoop and Apache Spark enable distributed processing across clusters of computers.

2.3. Data Science
The amount of data that is being generated by organizations from different sources
is growing exponentially. In order to properly utilize such an enormous amount
of data in a manner that ensures competitiveness, an appropriate type of approach
or method on how to use data is required. Data science provides the tools and
techniques that are used by organizations to take advantage of the vast amount of
data that they own.
Data Science is a blend of tools, algorithms, and machine learning (ML) principles
used to discover and extract hidden patterns. It is an interdisciplinary field that

The concept of big data is strongly associated with data science. Big data refers to
a huge volume of data that cannot be processed using traditional methods, and it
is characterized by volume, variety, and velocity. For example, in 2020 Facebook
generated 4 petabytes of data every day — that is a million gigabytes. Data science

big data.
