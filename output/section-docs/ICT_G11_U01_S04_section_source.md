## LMS LESSON (authoritative)
LEAD

At the heart of all information technology is a deceptively simple question: what is information? The DIKW hierarchy organises four related but distinct concepts in a hierarchical structure. Each level is built upon and enriched from the level below it.

PARAGRAPHS

Data — the foundation — consists of raw, unprocessed facts and figures that have no inherent meaning on their own. The number "37" is data. A list of numbers such as "250, 100, 375, 200" is data. Data can take many forms: numbers, text, images, audio recordings, sensor readings. Without context, data is meaningless. Ethiopia's mobile network operators generate millions of call detail records every day — each record is a piece of data.

Information is created when data is processed, organised, and given context so that it becomes meaningful and useful. Information answers questions such as "who?", "what?", "when?", and "where?" A bank statement is information: it takes raw transaction data and presents it in an organised way that shows a financial position. The process of converting data into information typically involves sorting, classifying, calculating, summarising, and formatting.

Knowledge represents the third level and is considerably richer than information alone. Knowledge emerges when a person combines information with experience, understanding, and reasoning to build a deeper comprehension of how things work. Knowledge allows us to understand not just "what happened" but "why it happened" and "what is likely to happen next." A meteorologist who has studied climate patterns for many years possesses knowledge that allows them to interpret new information and make reliable predictions. In IT, knowledge may be encoded in expert systems, recommendation engines, or machine learning models.

Wisdom, at the apex, is the capacity to use knowledge, experience, and good judgement to make the best possible decisions — particularly in complex or uncertain situations. Wisdom involves knowing not just what can be done, but what should be done and why. It incorporates ethical considerations, long-term thinking, and awareness of unintended consequences. A wise technology policymaker in Ethiopia does not simply know that 5G networks are technically superior to 4G. They understand which infrastructure investments are most appropriate for Ethiopia's current stage of development, economic constraints, and social needs.

The DIKW hierarchy was first formally described by information scientist Russell Ackoff in 1989. In Ethiopia, Ethio Telecom processes over 40 million transactions per month. Converting this raw data into actionable information and knowledge is one of the country's greatest ICT — Information and Communication Technology challenges.

1.1.1 Data
Data is raw facts that cannot give meaning on its own. It is a representation of what
we see and perceive using things like letters and numbers. Facts such as 100, 250,
age, and Ethiopia are considered as data. Each of them does not give meaning on
its own. For example, 100 can be the weight of a person or the measurement of a
distance. Likewise, the word Ethiopia can be a country name or a person’s name. It
does not tell us any meaning unless it is related to other data.
Data is recorded and stored using numbers, texts, and images. Some data can be
represented better by numbers than texts. For example, age is better represented by
numbers than texts. Likewise, words such as name are only represented by text.
With the development of satellite technology, representation of data in the form
of image has become very popular. Thus, one form of data representation is better
than the other forms of representation, depending on the type of data and context.

1.1.3 Knowledge
Knowledge is an understanding which is acquired by further processing and
integrating different sets of information. It includes both understanding and skill.
For example, when you tell a child how to ride a bicycle, you are giving information.
This helps the child to understand how to ride a bicycle. When a child applies the
information in riding a bicycle, the child acquires the skills necessary to properly
ride a bicycle. Then the information becomes knowledge. Therefore,
knowledge is acquired by integrating information from different sources such as
reading books, engaging in different activities, discussing with other people, and

1.1.4 Wisdom
Wisdom is the ability to make wise decisions and judgments using one’s knowledge.
It is the highest level of human understanding. It is associated with good judgment
and actions that contribute to living well.
Wisdom is like love, intelligence, and politeness in that it is a good thing to have.
The more we have it, the better we become.

1.1.5 The Difference between Data, Information, Knowledge, and Wisdom
Data, information, knowledge, and wisdom can be hierarchically arranged where
data is placed at the bottom and wisdom is placed at the top of the pyramid. As
you move up in the pyramid, the value you get increases. The more data is enriched
with meaning and context, the more knowledge and insights we get from it. The top
of the pyramid is where we have turned the knowledge and insights into wisdom
which is the highest level of human intelligence.
The difference between data, information, knowledge, and wisdom can be further
explained as follows:
Data is anything represented as numbers, texts, and images. For example, 010101
or +251********* are data.
Information may be viewed as data put into context. It is an organized form of
data to provide meaning. For example, when we say that +251********* is a
person’s phone number, the data becomes information. Information is a set of data
that has already been processed, analysed, and structured in a meaningful way to
become useful.
Knowledge refers to understanding patterns, rules, or other mechanisms that serve
as a guide for our future actions. It emerges from further processing and integration
