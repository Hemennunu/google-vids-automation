## LMS LESSON (authoritative)
LEAD

Electronic Banking (E-banking): The delivery of banking services through electronic channels, primarily the internet and mobile networks. In Ethiopia, the adoption of mobile banking has been remarkable. CBE Birr, Dashen Bank's Amole service, and TeleBirr have together brought millions of previously unbanked Ethiopians into the formal financial system. Beyond consumer services, e-banking includes interbank electronic fund transfer systems and automated clearing houses.

PARAGRAPHS

Electronic Government (E-government): The use of digital technologies to deliver government services more efficiently, transparently, and conveniently. Services are categorised by direction of interaction: Government-to-Citizen (G2C) services such as online birth registration or tax filing; Government-to-Business (G2B) services such as business registration and customs clearance; and Government-to-Government (G2G) services such as inter-ministerial data sharing. Ethiopia has made substantial investments including the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) and the Digital ID programme.

Electronic Learning (E-learning): Education and training delivered or supported through electronic media. E-learning platforms allow students to access lecture videos, interactive exercises, quizzes, and discussion forums from any location with an internet connection. Ethiopia's EduTV programme broadcasts educational content via satellite television. MyMarian Platform exemplifies how tailored e-learning solutions are designed specifically for the Ethiopian and broader African educational context.

Electronic Health (E-health): ICT — Information and Communication Technology — used to support and improve healthcare delivery. Applications include Electronic Health Records (EHR) systems, telemedicine platforms allowing patients in remote areas to consult with doctors in cities, and Health Management Information Systems (HMIS) that aggregate disease surveillance data. In Ethiopia, DHIS2 is used by the Ministry of Health to collect and analyse health data from thousands of health facilities across the country, enabling evidence-based policy decisions.

Cambridge Perspective — Cambridge AS Level IT 9626: The Cambridge syllabus identifies seven key characteristics of good information: accuracy (free from errors), completeness (all required data present), timeliness (available when needed), relevance (appropriate for the task), accessibility (reachable by authorised users), clarity (presented in an understandable format), and cost-effectiveness (value exceeds cost of obtaining). Be able to evaluate given scenarios against all seven characteristics. The Cambridge syllabus also examines Porter's Value Chain model, which analyses how IS can add value at each stage of an organisation's primary and support activities.

ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) integrates all core business processes — finance, HR, procurement, production, inventory, and customer relations — into a single unified software platform with a shared database. Major ERP vendors include SAP, Oracle, and Microsoft Dynamics. In Ethiopia, Ethiopian Airlines and Ethio Telecom use ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning systems.

CRM (Customer Relationship Management) systems help organisations manage interactions with current and potential customers. A CRM — Customer Relationship Management system — stores comprehensive data on each customer: contact information, purchase history, service interactions, and preferences. It provides tools for sales, marketing, and customer service. Leading CRM platforms include Salesforce, HubSpot, and Microsoft Dynamics 365.

SCM (Supply Chain Management) systems help organisations plan, manage, and optimise the flow of goods, services, information, and finances from raw material suppliers through production and distribution to the final customer. The Ethiopian Commodity Exchange (ECX) is an example of how digital IS have been used to bring transparency and efficiency to Ethiopia's agricultural commodity markets, benefiting both farmers and buyers.

Your Future in Ethiopia: The demand for ICT — Information and Communication Technology professionals who understand information systems is growing rapidly. The Digital Ethiopia 2025 strategy identifies priority areas: e-government services, digital financial inclusion, agri-tech, health informatics, and smart city development. Careers in IS analysis, digital project management, ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning implementation, and IT consulting are among the most sought-after positions in Ethiopia's emerging tech sector.

E-government, or electronic government, refers to the use of information and communication technologies by government agencies to deliver services, exchange information, communicate with citizens, and conduct administrative functions. In Ethiopia, the implementation of e-government has gained significant momentum through initiatives such as WoredaNet, a wide-area network connecting woreda (district) administrations across the country, providing video conferencing facilities and data connectivity. The National ID Programme (Fayda) represents another landmark e-government application, issuing digital identification numbers to Ethiopian citizens to streamline access to public services. The Ethiopian Ministry of Innovation and Technology continues to expand e-government services, including online business registration, tax filing through the Ethiopian Revenue Service, and passport application portals.

E-government services are typically categorised into several models. Government-to-Citizen (G2C) services provide citizens with online access to information, forms, and applications, reducing the need for physical visits to government offices. Government-to-Business (G2B) services facilitate business registration, licensing, tax payments, and procurement opportunities, improving the ease of doing business. Government-to-Government (G2G) services enable data sharing and coordination among different government agencies, enhancing efficiency and reducing duplication. Government-to-Employee (G2E) services support internal administrative functions such as payroll, leave management, and performance evaluation for civil servants. In Ethiopia, the Integrated Financial Management Information System (IFMIS) exemplifies G2G and G2E applications, enabling the Ministry of Finance to manage budget execution, accounting, and reporting across all government entities.

Telemedicine represents one of the most impactful applications of information systems in healthcare, particularly for developing nations like Ethiopia where access to medical specialists is limited in rural areas. Telemedicine uses information and communication technologies to provide clinical healthcare at a distance, enabling patients in remote health centres to consult with specialists in urban hospitals through video conferencing, store-and-forward imaging, and remote patient monitoring. In Ethiopia, the Ministries of Health and Innovation and Technology have piloted telemedicine projects connecting rural health centres with specialists at Black Lion Hospital in Addis Ababa, enabling remote diagnosis and treatment planning for patients who would otherwise need to travel long distances for specialist care. Teleconference systems, closely related to telemedicine, enable real-time audio and video communication among participants at different locations, supporting business meetings, distance education, and collaborative work across geographic boundaries.

E-commerce and e-banking have transformed the Ethiopian business landscape. Services such as Ethio Telecom's Telebirr mobile money platform, the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia's e-banking services, and various fintech applications have brought banking services to millions of previously unbanked Ethiopians. EthioJobs exemplifies how information systems have revolutionised the employment market by connecting job seekers with employers through an online platform. The Ethiopian Commodity Exchange (ECX) uses sophisticated information systems to facilitate transparent agricultural commodity trading, providing price discovery and market information to farmers across the country. These applications demonstrate how information systems can drive economic development, improve service delivery, and enhance quality of life when adapted to local contexts and needs.

1.3.1 E-Government
Electronic government or e-Government is the use of information systems
to provide electronic services to citizens with the purpose of increasing
efficiency, transparency, and citizen participation. The appropriate application
of e-Government allows for higher levels of effectiveness and efficiency in
governmental tasks. It also improves efficiency of communication among different
governmental offices. Different organizations establish an organizational portal to
facilitate communication between the organizations and their customers. A portal
is a web-based information system that collects information from different sources
into a single user interface so that users can easily access the organization’s
electronic services. Figure 1.9 shows the portal of the Ethiopian Ministry of
Health. The portal provides different health related services to citizens.
Citizens go to municipalities for business registrations, auto registry, payment of
taxes, issuance of birth certificates, issuance and renewal of identification cards,
etc. All forms of municipal services require citizens to go to the municipalities
in person. All these services can, however, be automated and made accessible for
citizens over the municipality’s web portal.
Adoption of e-government solution brings several benefits. Some of the benefits
are:
• Establishing trust between governments and citizens

• Allowing citizens to easily get government decisions and policies through

1.3.2 E-banking
In the traditional banking service, customers must go to the nearby banks to
withdraw money, transfer money to families, and pay their utility bills. The
traditional banking service is inconvenient and inefficient as it demands customers
to go the bank in person and wait their turns to get a service. It also creates
unnecessary burden on the bank.
Electronic banking is a form of technology-based banking service that avoids
exchange of cash, checks, or other types of paper documents. In the banking
sector, IT is mainly associated with services like Internet banking, Automated
Teller Machine (ATM) and mobile banking. Like in other service sectors, IT has
positively influenced the banking industry. Banks make their information systems
accessible over the Internet so as to provide Internet banking services. This allows customers to make payments online, transfer money from their
account to other accounts and get other banking services from wherever they are.

Some of the commonly known e-banking services include Automatic Teller
Machine (ATM), Mobile Banking, Internet Banking, Credit cards, Debit Cards,
Smart Cards, Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT) System, etc.
E-banking provides many benefits to banks as well as to customers. Some of the
benefits include:
• The cost of operation per unit of services is lower for banks.
• Customers can withdraw cash any time using, for example, ATM service.
• Customers can make payments from wherever they are.
• Customers can easily check their account balance
• Customers can get immediate message about transactions related to their
accounts.
Despite all of the several benefits that e-banking has, there are some challenges to
it such as:
• Digital theft
• Exclusion of the digitally illiterate citizen from e-banking services
• Lack of access to IT infrastructure in remote areas
• Unaffordable cost to acquire IT devices like smart phones for low-income
citizens

1.3.3 E-learning
The face-to-face form of education is the dominant form of educational delivery
but it has some limitations. It does not allow students to learn in their own pace,
and class schedules are not flexible. E-learning is introduced as an alternative form
of educational delivery.
E-learning is a form of learning delivered through the use of digital devices. A
number of other terms are also used to describe e-learning. It is known by terms
like online learning, virtual learning, distributed learning, computer-based
learning, etc. Despite their subtle differences, these terms in general refer to the
use of information and communication technologies for facilitating the teaching
learning process. If e-learning is provided over the Internet, it is referred to as
online learning
The main components of e-learning are the Internet, computers or mobile phones,
digital learning resources, content developers (such as teachers and graphic editors),
and learners.
Some of the benefits that e-learning offers to learners are:
• Flexibility in learning time: Learners can access and learn at any time, or
any day.

• Implementation of student-centered learning: Learners can learn on their
own pace.
• Access to a variety of educational resources from different schools or

• Rich multimedia learning resources: The multimedia e-learning content

• Reduced educational costs: there are no costs for travel and accommodation.
The main disadvantages of e-learning are:
• Physical isolation of students from teachers and other students

1.3.4 E-commerce
E-commerce (electronic commerce) is the buying and selling of goods and services
over the Internet. E-commerce is fundamentally a website deployed over Internet
to handle online transactions. Buyers go to the E-commerce website using their
smartphones or computers when they make an order for a product or service over
the Internet. Once the order is received by the merchant, the payment will be
processed through the Bank. After the whole transaction is successfully completed,
the order will be delivered to the buyer. In E-commerce transactions, many actors
are involved including merchants, buyers or customers, banks and transporters..

1.3.5 Telemedicine
Telemedicine refers to the practice of caring for patients remotely without the
caregiver and the patient being in the same physical location. Telemedicine was
originally introduced to treat patients who were in remote places, far away from
local health facilities, or in areas with shortages of medical professionals. People
who live in rural areas of Ethiopia do not have access to quality health services. In
addition to creating access, telemedicine has now been used as an alternative mode
of health service and as a tool for convenient medical care.
There are now different telemedicine companies that provide online treatment. A
patient can simply come online and request a visit with one of the telemedicine
company’s doctors and get treatment. The patients pay the service fee online
through their bank account. This online medical consultation has
already been implemented in Ethiopia. One can meet online doctors in Ethiopia by
browsing the Internet.
There are now new mobile-based medical services. Patients are starting to use
their mobile to monitor and track their health conditions. Simple home-use medical
devices allow patients to gather needed medical information for doctors (see
Figureure 1.17). These devices are used to measure the patient’s body temperature,
glucose levels, and blood pressure without the need to go to clinics. This encourages

Some of the advantages of telemedicine for patients include:
• Overcoming geographical barriers to access health care services
• Reduced cost through avoidance of costs such as travel and hotel services.
• Access to specialized physicians by people who live in remote locations
Telemedicine, however, has some limitations. Telemedicine requires technical
trainings and equipment, which may not be readily available in the rural areas of
developing countries.
