# Unit 5: Unit Summary



## Definition

The summary map reviews the unit's maintenance cycle: care for software, improve performance, check networks, prevent faults, and keep records.



## Lead

In this unit, you have learnt about the essential concepts and practices of computer maintenance and troubleshooting. The unit began with installing and uninstalling software, where you explored the different types of installations including clean, upgrade, and silent installations, as well as the importance of proper uninstallation to maintain system health. You also learned about software licensing and the guidelines provided by the Ethiopian Ministry of Innovation and Technology for educational institutions.



## Paragraphs

You studied software troubleshooting, gaining an understanding of common software problems such as freezing, crashes, and compatibility issues. The systematic troubleshooting process of identifying, isolating, fixing, and verifying was introduced, along with essential tools including the Task Manager, System Configuration utility (msconfig), and Safe Mode. You learned how to apply these techniques in the context of Ethiopian school computer laboratories, where hardware resources may be limited.

The unit covered tools for speeding up a computer system, including Disk Defragmenter, Disk Cleanup, Windows Update, System Restore, and the importance of regular maintenance schedules. You explored network troubleshooting, learning about common network issues such as connectivity loss, slow performance, and DNS failures, and how to use command-line tools including ipconfig, ping, tracert, and nslookup to diagnose these problems.

Finally, you were introduced to network maintenance tools, both software and hardware. The hardware tools discussed included wire crimpers, cable testers, punch-down tools, tone generators, and multimeters. The importance of preventive maintenance and performance monitoring was emphasised as a strategy for reducing downtime and extending the life of network equipment.

The skills you have developed in this unit are practical and immediately applicable. Whether you are maintaining a home computer, a school laboratory, or an office network, the systematic approach to troubleshooting and the knowledge of maintenance tools will serve you well. As technology continues to evolve, the ability to diagnose and resolve problems independently is a valuable skill that will benefit you throughout your academic and professional life.

UNIT-5 Maintenance and Troubleshooting
Wire Crimpers - A wire crimper (sometimes
called a cable crimper) is a tool that attaches media
connectors to the ends of cables. You can use it to
make or modify network cables (See Figure 5.23).
Cable Testers — A cable tester (sometimes called
a line tester) is a tool that verifies if a signal is
transmitted by a given cable. You can use one to
find out whether the cables in your network are
functioning properly when diagnosing connectivity
issues (See Figure 5.24).
Punch Down Tool — A punch-down tool is used
in a wiring closet to connect cable wires directly
to a patch panel or punch-down block. This tool
makes it easier to connect wires than it would be to
do it by hand (See Figure 5.25).
Tone Generator — A tone generator is a device
that sends an electrical signal through one pair
of UTP wires. You can use these tools to verify
that signals are passing through the wires in your
network. They are often used to confirm phone
connectivity ( See Figure 5.26).

Key Terms
Disk defragmentation - is the process of reorganizing the data stored on the
hard drive so that related pieces of data are put back together, all lined up in
a continuous fashion.
Disk Cleanup - Disk Cleanup helps free up space on your hard disk, creating
improved system performance. Disk Cleanup searches your disk and then
shows you temporary files, Internet cache files, and unnecessary program
files that you can safely delete.
Windows Update- keeps your computer up to date with security and bug
fixes automatically, with little effort on your end. This protects your computer
from malicious software and fixes known Windows bugs and other issues.
Installing software - is the act of making the program ready for execution.
Uninstalling Software – is a process of removing a program from your
system which you previously installed in your system.
System Restore - is a feature in Microsoft Windows that allows the user to
revert their computer’s state (including system files, installed applications,
Windows Registry, and system settings) to that of a previous point in time,
which can be used to recover from system malfunctions or other problems.
Troubleshooting - is a systematic approach to locating the fault part and
identifying the cause of the fault in a computer system.
Network troubleshooting – is the act of discovering and correcting problems
with connectivity, performance, security, and other aspects of networks.
